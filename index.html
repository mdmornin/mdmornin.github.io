I study how alcohol changes the way the brain computes. Using large-scale in vivo electrophysiology in behaving rodents, I ask how acute intoxication and developmental alcohol exposure reshape the neural circuits that support decision making, memory, and spatial navigation.

I am a postdoctoral fellow with David Linsenbardt and Fernando Valenzuela at UNM, where I record from the anterior thalamus, hippocampus, and surrounding regions in mouse models of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder. I completed my PhD in Addiction Neuroscience with Christopher Lapish at IUPUI, studying how ethanol alters prefrontal cortex population dynamics. Much of my work sits where experiments meet analysis: high-channel-count recordings, spike-sorting pipelines, and dimensionality-reduction approaches to neural population activity.

Research

Developmental alcohol & memory circuits

How third-trimester-equivalent alcohol exposure alters anterior thalamic, hippocampal, and contextual fear networks, and what that means for spatial encoding and memory in FASD.

Acute alcohol & prefrontal dynamics

Ethanol changes the variability, not just the rate, of prefrontal firing. I use state-space and population-level analyses to describe how intoxication reshapes cortical computation.

Decision making & cognitive control

Neural signatures of proactive versus reactive control, delay discounting, and compulsion-like drinking in the prefrontal, cingulate, and insular cortices.

Methods for large-scale ephys

Neuropixels and silicon-probe recordings, reproducible spike-sorting pipelines (SpikeInterface, Kilosort), Bonsai workflows for synchronised data streams, and NWB.
